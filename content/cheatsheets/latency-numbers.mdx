## Source and how to read it

The table below is the list popularised by Jeff Dean's talks at Google (for example his 2009 LADIS keynote) and Peter Norvig's essay *Teach Yourself Programming in Ten Years*, in the widely copied form that adds SSD rows. The figures are from roughly **2009–2012 hardware** and are **orders of magnitude, not measurements**: any real machine will differ by 2–10× on several rows. Colin Scott's interactive page ("Latency Numbers Every Programmer Should Know", Berkeley) re-projects the same list year by year from hardware trend lines; its later values are extrapolations, not benchmarks. Memorise the **ratios** and the **ladder**, not the digits.

## The ladder in one line

**ns** for CPU caches and memory → **µs** for SSD and in-rack network → **ms** for a data-centre round trip (0.5), disk seeks (10) and cross-continent trips (150). Each rung is roughly 1,000× the one below.

## Ratios to carry into a design

| Comparison | Ratio | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Memory vs SSD random read | 100 ns vs 150 µs ≈ **1:1,000** | Cache hot data in RAM (Redis, in-process) |
| Same-DC vs cross-continent round trip | 0.5 ms vs 150 ms = **1:300** | CDNs, regional replicas, fewer round trips per page |
| Sequential vs random on HDD | 20 ms per MB vs 10 ms per seek | Logs, LSM trees and batching are built around sequential I/O |
| In-process call vs network hop | ns vs ≥ 0.5 ms | Every microservice hop costs at least a DC round trip |

## The speed-of-light floor

Light in optical fibre travels at about **200,000 km/s** (refractive index ≈ 1.5), so **1 ms ≈ 200 km one way**, or **100 km of distance per 1 ms of round trip**.

- San Francisco ↔ Amsterdam is about 8,800 km great-circle: 2 × 8,800 ÷ 200,000 = **88 ms** minimum round trip. Real paths are longer and add routing, so the table's 150 ms is typical.
- A TLS 1.3 connection on a fresh TCP connection costs 2 round trips before the first request byte: about **300 ms** on that path. Connection reuse and edge termination (CDN) remove most of it.
- Synchronous replication across regions pays at least one round trip per commit: tens to 150 ms. Within a region across zones it is about a millisecond or two.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not quote digits as facts">
In an interview say "about half a millisecond for a round trip inside a data centre, about 150 ms across an ocean and back". Precise-sounding numbers from an old table invite a challenge you cannot win; ratios do not.
</Callout>
