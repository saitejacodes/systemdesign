## Method in four lines

1. State assumptions out loud (DAU, actions per user, object size, retention, peak factor).
2. Round to one significant figure and powers of ten.
3. Compute peak reads/s, writes/s, storage over retention, bandwidth, working set.
4. End with a sentence that changes the design: "writes are 40/s, so one primary is plenty; effort goes to the read path".

## Powers of two

| Power | Exact | Approx. | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2¹⁰ | 1,024 | 10³ | 1 KB (KiB) |
| 2²⁰ | 1,048,576 | 10⁶ | 1 MB (MiB) |
| 2³⁰ | 1,073,741,824 | 10⁹ | 1 GB (GiB) |
| 2⁴⁰ | ≈ 1.1 × 10¹² | 10¹² | 1 TB (TiB) |
| 2⁵⁰ | ≈ 1.13 × 10¹⁵ | 10¹⁵ | 1 PB (PiB) |
| 2¹⁶ | 65,536 | 6.5 × 10⁴ | Ports, uint16 |
| 2³² | ≈ 4.3 × 10⁹ | 4 × 10⁹ | IPv4 addresses, uint32 |
| 2⁶⁴ | ≈ 1.8 × 10¹⁹ | 2 × 10¹⁹ | uint64, 64-bit IDs |

The KB vs KiB gap is 2.4% at KB and 10% at TB; ignore it in an interview.

## Time conversions

| Quantity | Exact | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Seconds per day | 86,400 | **≈ 10⁵** |
| Seconds per 30-day month | 2,592,000 | ≈ 2.5 × 10⁶ |
| Seconds per year | 31,536,000 | ≈ 3 × 10⁷ |
| 1 million per day | 10⁶ ÷ 86,400 | ≈ **12/s** |
| 1 billion per day | 10⁹ ÷ 86,400 | ≈ **12,000/s** |
| 1 million per month | 10⁶ ÷ 2.59 × 10⁶ | ≈ **0.4/s** |

Dividing by 10⁵ instead of 86,400 understates per-second rates by about 14%. Peak factors dwarf that, so it is fine for sizing; say "about".

## Size conversions

| Product | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 KB × 1 million | 1 GB |
| 1 KB × 1 billion | 1 TB |
| 1 MB × 1 million | 1 TB |
| 1 MB × 1 billion | 1 PB |
| 1 Gbps | 125 MB/s (divide bits by 8) |
| 10 Gbps | 1.25 GB/s |

Typical sizes to assume: ID or timestamp 8 B; UUID 16 B (36 as text); short text post with metadata about 1 KB; JSON API response 1–10 KB; compressed photo 200 KB–2 MB; a minute of 1080p video about 10–50 MB depending on bitrate.

## Formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Average QPS | DAU × actions per user per day ÷ 10⁵ |
| Peak QPS | average × peak factor (2–3× global, 5–10× regional or event-driven) |
| Read and write QPS | split by the read:write ratio |
| Storage | new objects per day × size × days retained × replicas (+20–50% for indexes and metadata) |
| Bandwidth | QPS × bytes per request, separately for ingress and egress |
| Cache working set | distinct hot items per day × item size (often the top 10–20% of keys) |
| Servers | peak QPS ÷ (per-server QPS × target utilisation, e.g. 0.6) |
| Concurrency (Little's law) | in-flight = arrival rate × time in system |
| Shards | max(storage ÷ per-node storage, write QPS ÷ per-node write QPS) |
| Partitions (Kafka) | peak events/s ÷ per-consumer events/s, plus headroom |

## Worked lines

**Social app, 10M DAU × 20 requests/day:** 10⁷ × 20 = 2 × 10⁸/day ÷ 10⁵ = **2,000 QPS** average; × 3 peak = **6,000**; 90% reads → 5,400 reads/s and 600 writes/s at peak.

**URL shortener, 100M links/month, 100 reads per write, 5-year retention, 5× peak:**

| Line | Arithmetic | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Writes | 100M ÷ 2,592,000 s | **38.6/s**, call it 40 |
| Reads | 38.6 × 100 | 3,860/s; total ≈ 3,900/s |
| Peak | 3,900 × 5 | **19,500/s** |
| Links stored | 100M × 12 × 5 | **6 billion** |
| ID space, 7 chars base62 | 62⁷ | 3.52 trillion; 6B uses 0.17% |
| Storage | 6B × 500 B | **3 TB**, 9 TB with 3 replicas |
| Working set | 30M links/day × 500 B | **15 GB**, one cache cluster |

**Photo uploads, 1M/day × 2 MB:** storage 2 TB/day × 365 ≈ **730 TB/year** before replicas and thumbnails; ingress 10⁶ ÷ 10⁵ = 10/s × 2 MB = **20 MB/s** average.

**Timeline, 200M DAU, 10 loads and 0.5 posts per user per day, 3× peak:** writes 10⁸/day → 1,000/s, **3,000/s** peak; reads 2 × 10⁹/day → 20,000/s, **60,000/s** peak; text 100 GB/day ≈ 36 TB/year; egress 20,000 × 20 posts × 1 KB = **400 MB/s** before images.

**Kafka clickstream, 50,000 events/s × 1 KB, consumer 2,000/s:** 25 consumers → ≥ 25 partitions (choose 48 for headroom); 50 MB/s ingest; 7-day retention 50 MB/s × 604,800 s ≈ **30 TB**, ≈ **91 TB** at replication factor 3.

**Little's law:** 300 req/s to a dependency with 1 s timeouts during an outage → 300 × 1 = **300** threads stuck waiting.

## Interview habits

- Write units on every number; most estimation errors are unit errors (bits vs bytes, per day vs per second).
- Keep it to 2–3 minutes and end with a conclusion. Offer more depth if the interviewer wants it.
- Size for peak, store for retention, cache for the working set.
