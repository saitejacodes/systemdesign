# Travel and Expenses

## Principles

Spend company money as if it were your own, and only when travel is the best way to get the work done. A video call is often enough. When you do travel, choose the option that gives the best value overall, which is not always the cheapest ticket.

## Approval before travel

All business travel must be approved by your manager before you book. International travel also needs approval from your director. Book flights, trains and hotels through the company travel desk so that we know where people are if there is an emergency; bookings made elsewhere are only refunded if they were at least ten percent cheaper than the travel desk price for the same journey.

## Flights and trains

Economy class is the standard for all flights. Premium economy is allowed for a single flight segment longer than six hours. Business class is only allowed for flights longer than ten hours when you are expected to work on the day you land, and it needs director approval in writing. For journeys under four hours, take the train where a direct train exists.

## Hotels

Hotels must be booked through the travel desk within the nightly cap for the city. The cap is 160 per night in most cities and 220 per night in the high-cost cities listed in the portal. If the only hotels near a customer site are above the cap, the travel desk can approve an exception before you book.

## Daily allowance

Instead of claiming every meal, travellers receive a daily allowance, called the per-diem, which covers meals, drinks and small incidental costs. The per-diem depends on the destination zone. Zone A covers domestic travel and pays 45 per day. Zone B covers most of Europe and pays 60 per day. Zone C covers North America, Japan, Singapore and Australia and pays 80 per day. If a customer or conference provides a meal, reduce the per-diem by 15 for each meal provided. On the day you travel out and the day you travel back, you receive 75 percent of the per-diem.

## Mileage

If you use your own car for business, claim mileage at 0.45 per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in a tax year and 0.25 per mile after that. Commuting between your home and your normal site is not business mileage. Parking and tolls are reimbursed with a receipt.

## Submitting claims

Submit expense claims with form EXP-7 within 60 days of the date you spent the money. Each item over 25 needs an itemised receipt; a photograph of the receipt is fine. Claims submitted later than 60 days are only paid if your director approves the delay. Approved claims are paid with the next monthly salary run if they are approved before the 15th of the month.

## Corporate cards

Employees who travel more than six times a year may apply for a corporate card. The corporate card must only be used for business costs. If a personal cost is charged to the card by mistake, report it within seven days and the amount will be deducted from your next salary.

## What we do not reimburse

We do not pay for upgrades you choose yourself, minibar items, traffic or parking fines, personal entertainment, or lost luggage that is covered by the airline. Alcohol is only reimbursed as part of an approved client meal.
