# Health and Safety

## Everyone's responsibility

Everyone is responsible for working safely and for not putting others at risk. You have the right and the duty to stop any work you believe is unsafe. Stopping work for safety reasons will never count against you, even if it turns out there was no danger.

## Reporting accidents and near misses

Report every accident, injury or near miss through the safety reporting form HS-1 before the end of your shift. A near miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not, such as a dropped tool that landed next to someone. Near misses are the most useful reports we get, because they let us fix a problem before anyone is hurt.

## First aid

Each site has trained first aiders, listed on the noticeboard by every exit. First aid kits are in every kitchen and on every factory line. Eye wash stations are in the laboratory and next to the battery assembly area.

## Personal protective equipment

Safety glasses and safety shoes are required on the factory floor at all times. Hearing protection is required in the test hall when the shaker table is running, which is shown by an amber light above the door. The company provides all required protective equipment free of charge; prescription safety glasses are provided up to a cost of 150 per year.

## The calibration laboratory

The calibration laboratory uses certified reference gases, some of which are toxic or flammable. Only staff who hold a current gas handling certificate may connect or change gas cylinders. Two people must be present whenever a toxic reference gas is in use, and the fixed gas monitors in the laboratory must be checked at the start of every shift. If a fixed monitor alarms, leave the laboratory, close the door and call the site safety lead; do not go back in to switch anything off.

## Lone working

Lone working in the factory or the laboratory outside normal hours is only allowed for tasks on the approved lone-working list, and only after you have checked in with security. Security will call you every hour; if you do not answer, they will come to find you.

## Display screen equipment

If you work at a screen for more than an hour a day, complete the workstation assessment in the portal. Employees who need glasses specifically for screen work can claim the cost of an eye test and up to 80 towards glasses.

## Driving for work

If you drive for work, your licence is checked every year. Do not take calls while driving, even hands-free, unless the call is an emergency. Plan journeys so that you take a 15-minute break at least every two hours.

## Stress and wellbeing

Workload pressure is a health and safety matter too. If your workload is making you unwell, tell your manager or the People Team; they can complete a stress risk assessment with you and agree changes.
