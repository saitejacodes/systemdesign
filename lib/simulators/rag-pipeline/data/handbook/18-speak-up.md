# Speaking Up, Grievances and Discipline

## Speaking up about wrongdoing

If you believe something seriously wrong is happening at Brindlewood, such as fraud, a danger to health or safety, a breach of the law, falsified test results or a cover-up of any of these, you should speak up. You can raise it with your manager, with a director, or through the independent speak-up line, which is run by an outside provider and can be used anonymously by phone or online.

## Protection for people who speak up

Nobody may be dismissed, disciplined, refused promotion or treated worse because they raised a concern in good faith, even if the concern turns out to be mistaken. Retaliating against someone who has spoken up is itself a disciplinary offence. If you think you are being treated badly because you spoke up, tell the General Counsel directly.

## What happens next

Concerns raised through the speak-up line are passed to the General Counsel and the chair of the audit committee, never to the person the concern is about. You will receive an acknowledgement within seven days and an update on progress within 30 days, although we cannot always share the outcome if it involves other people's confidential information.

## Raising a grievance

A grievance is a concern or complaint about your own work, working conditions or relationships with colleagues. Try to resolve it informally with your manager first. If that does not work, or the grievance is about your manager, raise a formal grievance in writing with the People Team using form HR-400. A grievance meeting will be held within ten working days, and you may bring a colleague or a trade union representative.

## Grievance appeals

If you are unhappy with the outcome of a formal grievance, you may appeal in writing within ten working days of receiving the decision. The appeal is heard by a manager who was not involved in the original decision, and the appeal decision is final.

## The disciplinary procedure

Disciplinary action follows an investigation by someone not involved in the matter. You will be told in writing what the concern is and given the evidence before any disciplinary meeting. The possible outcomes are no action, a first written warning, a final written warning, or dismissal. Warnings stay on your record for twelve months for a first written warning and eighteen months for a final written warning.

## Gross misconduct

Some actions are serious enough to justify dismissal for a first offence. These include theft, fraud, violence, serious harassment, deliberate falsification of calibration or test records, working under the influence of alcohol or drugs in a safety-critical role, and serious breaches of the data classification policy. During an investigation into gross misconduct you may be suspended on full pay; suspension is not a punishment and does not mean a decision has been made.

## Appeals against disciplinary decisions

You may appeal any disciplinary outcome within five working days. Appeals are heard by a more senior manager who was not involved in the original decision.
