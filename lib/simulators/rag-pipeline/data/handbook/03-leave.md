# Leave and Time Off

## Annual leave entitlement

Full-time employees receive 25 days of paid annual leave per leave year, plus public holidays. The leave year runs from 1 April to 31 March. Part-time employees receive the same entitlement in proportion to the hours in their contract. After five complete years of service your entitlement rises to 27 days, and after ten complete years it rises to 30 days.

## Booking leave

Book leave in the portal as early as you can. Requests for up to three consecutive days need one week of notice; longer requests need notice of at least twice the length of the leave. Managers should answer a leave request within five working days. In the factory, no more than two people from the same line may be on leave on the same day, so popular weeks are allocated in the order requests arrive.

## Carrying leave over

You may carry over up to five days of unused annual leave into the next leave year. Carried-over days must be used by 30 June of the new leave year, after which they expire without payment. If you could not take leave because you were on long-term sick leave or parental leave, the five-day limit does not apply and your manager will agree a plan with the People Team.

## Buying and selling leave

Once a year, during the benefits window in February, you may buy up to five extra days of leave through salary sacrifice. Selling leave back to the company is not allowed, because rest is the point of leave.

## Public holidays

Each site observes the public holidays of its country. In addition, the whole company closes on the working days between 25 December and 1 January; these closure days are given on top of your annual leave and are not deducted from it. Staff who must work during the closure, such as the on-call engineers, receive a day of TOIL for each closure day worked.

## Sick leave

If you are ill, tell your manager before your start time on the first day. For absences of up to seven calendar days you can self-certify in the portal when you return. For absences longer than seven calendar days you need a doctor's note, uploaded with form HR-114. Company sick pay is full pay for the first 20 working days of sickness in any rolling twelve-month period, then half pay for the next 20 working days, after which statutory sick pay applies.

## Return to work

After any absence of more than two weeks, your manager will hold a short return-to-work conversation. The purpose is to check whether you need any adjustments, such as a phased return or different duties, and not to question whether you were ill.

## Parental leave

Birth parents receive 26 weeks of parental leave at full pay, followed by up to 26 more weeks of unpaid leave. Partners and adoptive parents receive 12 weeks at full pay. Parental leave at full pay requires six months of continuous service at the expected date of birth or placement. You can take partner leave in up to three blocks within the first year.

## Bereavement and compassionate leave

If a close family member dies, you may take up to ten days of paid bereavement leave. For the loss of a pregnancy, either parent may take up to ten days of paid leave. Managers may grant further compassionate leave for other serious family situations; it does not need to be justified in detail.

## Jury service and volunteering

Jury service is paid as normal working time. You also get two paid volunteering days per year to support a registered charity or a school.
