# Performance and Career Development

## The review cycle

Brindlewood runs two review conversations each year. The mid-year check-in happens in October and is a conversation about progress and support, with no rating. The annual review happens in April and includes a rating, which feeds into pay decisions. New starters who joined less than three months before a review are not rated in that cycle.

## Objectives

At the start of each review year, agree three to five objectives with your manager. At least one objective should be about learning or helping others learn. Objectives can change during the year when priorities change; record the change in the portal so that you are reviewed against what you were actually asked to do.

## Ratings

The annual rating uses four levels: Exceptional, Strong, Developing and Below expectations. Ratings are calibrated in a meeting of managers in each department, so that a Strong in one team means the same as a Strong in another. There is no quota for any rating.

## Feedback from colleagues

Before the annual review, you choose three to five colleagues to give written feedback. Your manager may add one more. Feedback is shared with you in summary, without names, unless the colleague chooses to sign it.

## Promotion

Promotions are decided twice a year, in April and October. Your manager nominates you with a short written case that shows you are already working at the next level for at least six months. A promotion panel from outside your immediate team reviews each case. If a nomination is not approved, the panel gives written feedback about what evidence was missing, and you may be nominated again in the next round.

## Career levels

Engineering and product roles follow the technical ladder from level T1 to T7, and people managers follow the management ladder from M3 to M6. Moving between ladders is a sideways move, not a promotion, and keeps your pay.

## Learning budget

Each employee has an annual learning budget of 1,200 for courses, conferences, books and certifications that are relevant to their role. Managers approve spending from the budget; anything over 600 in a single item also needs approval from the department head. Unused budget does not roll over to the next year. If the company pays more than 2,000 for a single certification and you leave within twelve months, you may be asked to repay part of the cost.

## Underperformance

If performance falls below expectations, your manager will first discuss it informally and agree support. If that does not help, a formal performance improvement plan of at least eight weeks is agreed in writing, with clear goals and regular check-ins.

Policy owner: Head of Talent.
