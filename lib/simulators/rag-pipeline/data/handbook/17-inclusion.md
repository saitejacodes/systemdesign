# Inclusion, Accessibility and Adjustments

## Our commitment

We want everyone to be able to do their best work here. That means hiring fairly, paying fairly, and removing barriers that make work harder for some people than for others.

## Workplace adjustments

If a disability, a long-term health condition or neurodivergence makes some part of your job harder, you can ask for a workplace adjustment. Examples include a different chair or screen, speech-to-text software, written agendas before meetings, a quieter desk, or changed working hours. You do not need a formal diagnosis to ask. Talk to your manager or the People Team, or use the adjustments form HR-150 if you prefer to write it down.

## How requests are handled

Your manager meets you to understand what would help, and may ask an occupational health adviser for advice with your consent. Simple adjustments, such as equipment under 500, should be in place within two weeks of the request. Adjustments are recorded in an adjustments passport, which you own and which moves with you if you change team, so that you do not have to explain your needs again.

## Recruitment

Every job advert says that candidates can ask for adjustments to the interview process. Interview panels include at least two people, and at least one of them has completed the fair hiring course. Interview questions are prepared in advance and every candidate for the same role is asked the same core questions.

## Pay equality

We publish a pay gap report every year. Each annual pay review includes a check for unexplained pay differences between people doing equal work, and any difference that cannot be explained is corrected in that review.

## Religious observance

You may use flexible hours or annual leave for religious festivals. Quiet rooms for prayer and reflection are available at Harrowgate and Velmont and can be used without booking.

## Menopause and reproductive health

Menopause symptoms, fertility treatment and pregnancy-related illness are treated with the same care as any other health matter. You may take paid time off for fertility treatment appointments, up to five appointments per treatment cycle.

## Employee networks

Employee networks run with company support and a small annual budget. Current networks include the disability and neurodiversity network, the parents and carers network, the women in engineering network and the pride network. Network leads may spend up to two hours a week of work time on network activity.

## Reporting discrimination

If you experience or see discrimination, report it to your manager, the People Team or through the speak-up line. Reports are investigated promptly and confidentially, and nobody may be treated badly for making a report in good faith.
