# Export Control and Working with Customers

## Why export control matters

Some of our products and their firmware can be used in sensitive industries, and a few of our high-sensitivity sensors are controlled items under export law. Shipping a controlled item, or even sharing controlled technical data, to the wrong destination is a criminal offence for the company and for the individuals involved.

## Classifying products

Every product and firmware release has an export classification, recorded in the product database by the trade compliance team. Engineers designing a new product must request a classification before the first prototype leaves the building, including prototypes sent to customers for trials.

## Screening customers and orders

Every new customer, distributor and end user is screened against the sanctions lists before a quote is issued. Orders are screened again before they ship, because the lists change often. If the screening tool flags a match, the order is stopped automatically and may only be released by the trade compliance team. Sales staff must never advise a customer on how to avoid a screening check.

## Sharing technical data

Technical drawings, source code and test data for controlled products may only be shared with people outside the company after trade compliance has confirmed that no licence is needed or that a licence has been granted. This includes sharing on video calls and at conferences. Visitors from other countries must not be shown controlled designs during site tours.

## Travelling with company equipment

Laptops containing controlled technical data must not be taken to countries on the restricted destinations list. If you need to travel to one of those countries, IT will issue a clean loan laptop with only the data you need. Demonstration units of controlled products may only travel with a licence or a confirmed exemption.

## Talking to customers about performance

Only quote performance figures that appear in the current published datasheet. If a customer asks whether an instrument will work outside its published range, say that you will find out, and pass the question to the application engineering team, who will answer in writing. Promises made verbally by sales staff about untested performance have caused serious problems in the past.

## Customer data

Data our instruments send to the Brindlewood cloud belongs to the customer. Engineers may access a customer's device data only to fix a problem the customer has reported, or with the customer's written permission, and every access is logged. Using customer data to develop new features requires a written agreement with that customer.

## Competitor information

You may use public information about competitors, such as their published datasheets. Do not ask new employees to share confidential information from their previous employer, and do not accept competitor information that someone offers you if it looks confidential. Report any such offer to Legal.

Policy owner: Trade Compliance Manager.
