# Communication and Meetings

## Choosing the right channel

Use chat for quick questions and coordination, email for decisions that people outside your team need to find later, and the documentation wiki for anything that should outlive the conversation. Decisions that change a product, a process or a customer commitment must be written down in the wiki or the ticket system, not left in chat.

## Response expectations

During core hours, aim to answer direct chat messages within half a day and email within two working days. Outside your working hours you are not expected to read or answer messages, unless you are on call. Use scheduled send when you write to colleagues late in the evening, so that you do not create pressure to reply.

## Meetings

Every meeting needs an agenda in the invitation and a named owner. Default meeting lengths are 25 or 50 minutes, so that people have time to move between rooms or take a break. If a meeting has no agenda, you may politely decline it. Friday afternoons are kept free of internal meetings wherever possible, to leave time for focused work.

## Recording meetings

Meetings may only be recorded if everyone attending is told at the start and nobody objects. Recordings and automatic transcripts are kept for 90 days and then deleted, unless the meeting owner moves them to the team folder because they are needed for longer. Meetings that discuss individual employees, such as performance or grievance meetings, are never recorded.

## Company updates

The chief executive holds an all-hands meeting on the first Tuesday of every month, broadcast to all three sites with live captions. Questions can be submitted in advance, anonymously if you prefer, and the most popular questions are answered first.

## Talking to the media

Only the communications team and people they have authorised may speak to journalists on behalf of Brindlewood. If a journalist contacts you, take their name and deadline, say that someone will get back to them, and pass the details to the communications team the same day.

## Language and tone

Write plainly. Prefer short sentences, explain abbreviations the first time you use them, and remember that many colleagues work in their second or third language. Disagree with ideas, not people.

Policy owner: Head of Communications.
